Here are somethings to consider when calculating thw point spread for two players:
- what is their overall record? Better players will have the best records
- When a player wins or losses, how much did they win or lose by? If they typically lose by a large margin then they are not very good. What counts as a large or small margin can be inferred from the data set. If the average margin of victory is 3, then losing by 17 is a bad loss
- How good where teams in their matchup. For example if player 1 played with the Giants and player 2 used the the Packers, player 1 would have a huge advantage. If player 2 won or kept the game very close it indicates in a matchup with even teams player 2 would have an advantage.
- does the player use the strengths of the teams, for example a good passing team against a week defense, are they able to get lots of passing yards. To determine if the yards passing we a lot or a little compare them to the average number in other games. (same goes for rushing yards)
- more recent matchups should weight heavier than older ones
- consider previous head to head matchups when calculating the spread. If they have played against each other a number of times with even teams and one player consistently beats the other that would tilt the spread in the winners direction
